Warning Signs

Common Internship Scam Red Flags

If you observe any of the following patterns, proceed with caution and do not transmit sensitive personal data or funds.

Payment for "Equipment" or "Training"

You are sent a fake check to purchase equipment from a specific vendor, or asked to pay an upfront onboarding fee.

No Formal Technical or Live Interview

Immediate job offers given after answering 3-4 simple questions in a Google Form or Telegram chat without video or voice verification.

Mismatched Corporate Domains

The recruiter claims to represent a Fortune 500 company but emails from @gmail.com or an address with subtle misspellings (e.g., @micros0ft-careers.com).

Verification Guide

Job Verification Checklist

Follow these practical steps to confirm whether an opportunity is authentic before accepting.

Verify the exact domain after the '@' symbol. Authentic recruiters communicate through official company domains (e.g. recruiter@google.com). Be alert for free public domains or subtle typo domains.
Navigate independently to the company's official careers or jobs page. Search for the job ID or title. If the position does not exist on their official portal, reach out to their HR department through their verified contact email.
No genuine company will ever ask a student to pay application fees, ID badge fees, or software installation charges. Any demand for upfront payment is an immediate confirmation of a scam.
